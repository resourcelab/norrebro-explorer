# Style exploration

Goal: choose the look before building a style pack. Same subject in every style so they compare fairly.

**Test scene:** Dronning Louises Bro and the lakes, looking along Nørrebrogade toward Sankt Hans Torv. A child
character in a yellow raincoat with a small map in hand, cyclists, autumn trees, the lake on one side.

Candidates: papercraft (exists, see `prototypes/assistens-paper-map/`), top-down 2D illustrated, pixel art.
Mockups drawn from the real map data go in this folder as they are made.

## Image prompts (paste into any image generator)

**Top-down 2D illustrated**
> Top-down illustrated map game scene of a Copenhagen neighbourhood: a stone bridge over a long city lake, wide street
> with bike lanes, five-storey ochre and red brick apartment buildings seen from above with tiled roofs, autumn trees,
> tiny cyclists, a child in a yellow raincoat holding a paper map. Hand-drawn picture-book style, soft gouache colours,
> clean outlines, gentle shadows, street names in small friendly lettering, cosy and readable, game UI-free.

**Pixel art**
> 16-bit pixel art top-down RPG view of a Copenhagen street by a city lake: a bridge with cyclists, brick apartment
> blocks with tiled roofs, autumn trees, cafés with awnings, a small child character in a yellow raincoat with a map.
> Limited warm palette, crisp pixels, no blur, cosy exploration game, readable tiles, game UI-free.

**Papercraft (for comparison)**
> Miniature pop-up paper diorama of a Copenhagen street by a lake, cut cardstock buildings and trees standing on a
> printed paper ground, a paper-doll child in a yellow raincoat with a map, tilt-shift photograph, warm low autumn sun,
> soft coloured shadows, handmade, tactile.

## How to choose

For each style: is it readable on a phone, does a kid instantly see streets vs buildings vs parks, can it show street
names, and how much work is a whole neighbourhood in it (pixel and 2D are much lighter than 3D papercraft).
